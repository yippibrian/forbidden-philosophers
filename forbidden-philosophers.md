# SECTION 1 — FOUNDATIONS / EL TRIÁNGULO DEL DESTINO ✅

## *(Pythagoras / Plato / Aristotle are introduced as roles, not yet questioned)*

---

### PROLOGUE

*(TOTAL DARKNESS.)*

*(A single DRUMBEAT.)*

*(Another.)*

*(A THIRD.)*

*(Suddenly: LIGHTNING. WIND. VIOLINS SCREAM.)*

**NARRATOR (too serious, practically vibrating):**
In an ancient land...
before the fall of empires...
before the rise of machines...
before anyone thought to say
"maybe we should slow down"...

there lived three men
who would ruin everything.

*(A beat.)*

Not because they were evil.

*(Another beat.)*

But because they were **right**
in incompatible ways.

*(THUNDER. CUT.)*

*(A clipboard clicks somewhere in the dark.)*

**CHORUS MEMBER (whispers, annoyed):**
That thunder was not approved in the current budget.

**ANOTHER CHORUS MEMBER (whispers):**
We’ll have to borrow it from later.

## SCENE 1 — THE ORACLE CANNOT HANDLE THIS (LA PROFECÍA IMPOSIBLE)

*(The ORACLE OF DELPHI convulses atop her tripod. Smoke. Incense. Too much incense. A GOAT wanders in, looking concerned.)*

**ORACLE'S ASSISTANT (panicked):**
She's shaking again!

**ORACLE (screaming):**
I SEE—
I SEE—
I SEE A WHEEL!

*(Lightning.)*

**ASSISTANT:**
A wheel of what, my lady?

**ORACLE:**
OF NUMBER!
OF FORM!
OF CATEGORIES THAT SHOULD NEVER HAVE BEEN INVENTED!

*(She pauses, gasps, starts again.)*

**ORACLE:**
NO WAIT—
I SEE THREE WHEELS!
NO—
ONE WHEEL WITH THREE SPOKES!
NO—
THREE MEN WHO THINK THEY'RE WHEELS!

**ASSISTANT (desperately taking notes):**
My lady, which vision should I—

**ORACLE:**
ALL OF THEM!
NONE OF THEM!
THE VISIONS CONTRADICT EACH OTHER
WHICH MEANS THEY'RE ALL TRUE!

*(The ground shakes. The GOAT faints. Someone in the audience drops a cup.)*

**ORACLE:**
THE GODS HAVE CHOSEN THREE!

**Assistant (hopeful):**
So... action items?

*(She points wildly.)*

ONE WHO WILL TURN THE WORLD
INTO NUMBER!

*(She spins, points again.)*

ONE WHO WILL TURN THE WORLD
INTO SHADOW!

*(She spins the other way, nearly falls off the tripod.)*

ONE WHO WILL TURN THE WORLD
INTO A LIST!

*(She stops spinning, looks directly at the audience.)*

**ORACLE:**
OR MAYBE THAT'S THE SAME PERSON!
THE SMOKE IS UNCLEAR!

**ASSISTANT:**
My lady, perhaps if you could—

**ORACLE (now whispering intensely):**
I also see...
a fish.
It will be important.
Don't ask me why.
The gods don't explain the fish.

*(Beat.)*

And a barrel.
And a chair.
¡Dios mío, SO MANY CHAIRS!

*(In the corner of the temple, a LARGE BOOK on a stand turns one page by itself. No one reacts.)*
*(A faint dust cloud rises. No one coughs.)*

**ASSISTANT (hesitant):**
...Is that bad?

**ORACLE:**
I SEE A MAN WHO ASKS TOO MANY QUESTIONS!

**ASSISTANT:**
That does sound bad—

**ORACLE:**
NO!
HE'S THE GOOD ONE!
OR THE WORST ONE!
THE FUTURE KEEPS CHANGING ITS MIND!

*(She clutches her head.)*

**ORACLE:**
Wait, there's more—
I see a tournament!
No, a trial!
No, a...
¿qué es eso?
A MACHINE?
A MACHINE THAT THINKS?

*(She convulses harder.)*

**ORACLE:**
IT'S ASKING ME QUESTIONS!
THE FUTURE IS INTERROGATING ME!
THIS HAS NEVER HAPPENED BEFORE!

**ASSISTANT (quietly, to the other priests):**
Should we... stop the prophecy?

**ORACLE (suddenly calm, terrifyingly so):**
No.
Let it come.

*(She looks at the assistant.)*

**ORACLE:**
Three men will create operations.
The operations will replace the men.
The men will become the operations.
And no one will remember
which came first.

**ASSISTANT (small voice):**
...Do we have funding for "operations replacing men"?

**ORACLE (staring, dead serious):**
No.

*(Long pause.)*

**ASSISTANT:**
...I don't understand, my lady.

**ORACLE (standing, robes billowing):**
Good.
That's the only honest response.

*(She looks at the audience one more time.)*

**ORACLE:**
Write this down exactly:
"They will build systems
to escape confusion.
The systems will CREATE confusion.
They will build new systems
to escape THAT confusion.
¡Y así sucesivamente, por toda la eternidad!"

*(Beat.)*

**ASSISTANT:**
Is... that bad?

**ORACLE (quiet, exhausted, terrified):**
Yes.

*(She collapses. The GOAT wakes up, sees her, faints again.)*

**ASSISTANT (to the other priests):**
...Should we warn someone?

**PRIEST #2:**
Who would we even tell?

**ASSISTANT:**
The three men?

**PRIEST #2:**
They'll think we're describing someone else.

*(BLACKOUT. Thunder. Somewhere, a violin weeps.)*

## SCENE 2 — PYTHAGORAS AND THE WHEEL (LA RUEDA CELESTIAL)

*(LIGHTS UP on a hidden temple. Candles. More candles. An OBSCENELY LARGE GEOMETRIC WHEEL painted on the floor. It glows faintly, as if lit from beneath.)*

*(PYTHAGORAS stands barefoot in the center, arms raised. DISCIPLES kneel in perfect geometric formation. The GOAT has somehow followed them here and sits in the corner, suspicious.)*

*(Wind begins to blow INSIDE THE TEMPLE for no physical reason.)*

**PYTHAGORAS (whispering, reverent):**
Brothers.
Sisters.
Regular polygons.

Today...
we do not count.

We **commune**.

**DISCIPLE #1 (trembling):**
Master... is it true the gods speak through numbers?

**PYTHAGORAS (whispering):**
No.

*(The disciples gasp. One touches their chest like they've been stabbed.)*

**PYTHAGORAS (suddenly SHOUTING):**
THE GODS *ARE* NUMBERS!

*(THUNDER CRASHES. Lightning strikes outside. The wheel GLOWS BRIGHTER. The goat runs in a circle.)*

**DISCIPLE #2 (weeping):**
Master! What is the holiest number?

**PYTHAGORAS (turning slowly, dramatically):**
SIX.

*(Everyone gasps so hard one disciple forgets to breathe and nearly passes out.)*

**DISCIPLE #3 (desperate):**
Why six?!
¡Díganos, Maestro!

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Because it is **perfect**.

*(He begins to pace the wheel.)*

Because it returns to itself.

*(One step.)*

Because it closes a cycle without residue.

*(Another step.)*

Because it knows when to stop.

*(He stops. The wheel pulses.)*

**DISCIPLE #2:**
Unless it doesn't...

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Six equals one plus two plus three.
Six equals one TIMES two TIMES three.
It is ADDITION and MULTIPLICATION
collapsed into the same truth!

*(A disciple sobs openly.)*

**DISCIPLE #1 (whispering):**
...What's a residue?

*(PYTHAGORAS stops. Turns. Stares into the distance like he's seeing through dimensions.)*

**PYTHAGORAS (softly, like a threat):**
A residue...
is what remains
when you refuse to understand the wheel.

*(Long, uncomfortable pause.)*

It is the chaos
that NUMBER
was invented to destroy.

*(He gestures. The wheel GLOWS.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Look upon it.

Not as a shape.

But as a **machine**.

*(Two disciples faint simultaneously. The goat backs toward the door.)*

**DISCIPLE #2:**
A... machine, master?

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Yes.
A machine that TURNS.
A machine that EXPLAINS.
A machine that NEVER STOPS.

*(The wheel begins to rotate on its own. No one is touching it.)*

**DISCIPLE #1 (awed):**
Master... is the wheel insured?

**PYTHAGORAS (offended):**
The gods don’t do insurance.

**PYTHAGORAS (arms wide, ecstatic):**
Do you see it?
DO YOU SEE IT?!

The cosmos is not chaos!
It is CYCLE!
¡Es una máquina perfecta!

And we...
WE...
are the priests of its turning!

*(Lightning STRIKES the center of the wheel. Everyone screams. The goat runs out the door.)*

*(A distant, bored voice from offstage): Put it on the repair list.)*

**PYTHAGORAS (laughing):**
YES!
THE GODS APPROVE!

*(Dramatic sting. Freeze frame on his face.)*

## SCENE 3 — PLATO ENTERS (AND MAKES IT WORSE)

*(SUDDEN WIND. ROMANTIC MUSIC—like a guitar ballad meeting a harp concerto. PLATO enters in SLOW MOTION, hair blowing even though they're indoors. Rose petals fall from nowhere.)*

*(The disciples turn. The wheel stops glowing. Even Pythagoras looks annoyed.)*

**PLATO (hand on heart, voice trembling with emotion):**
Pythagoras...

*(He pauses. The music swells. He waits for it.)*

...my old friend.

*(He looks at the wheel. His face falls.)*

Oh.

Oh no.

**PYTHAGORAS (defensive, territorial):**
Do not mock what you do not understand.

**PLATO (stepping closer, tragic):**
I understand it perfectly.

*(The disciples lean in. This is going to be good.)*

It is...

*(He touches the edge of the wheel gently, like it might break.)*

...a shadow.

*(Collective GASP. Someone clutches their robes. The goat pokes its head back in to watch.)*

**PYTHAGORAS (REELING):**
A SHADOW?!

**PLATO (looking upward, because of course he is):**
Yes.
A shadow of the True Wheel.
The Wheel that exists beyond turning.
Beyond chalk.
Beyond feet touching the ground.

*(He gestures upward dramatically.)*

In the Realm of Forms.

*(Someone sobs openly. Another disciple says "no, no, no" under their breath.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
My wheel is sacred!

**PLATO:**
All sacred things are imperfect copies.

*(DRAMATIC SLAP SOUND. No one knows who slapped whom. Everyone looks around.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
¡Eres un traidor!

**PLATO (hand still on chest):**
I do not betray.
I *elevate*.

*(He turns to the disciples.)*

Do you know what I see when I look at your wheel?

**PYTHAGORAS:**
ENLIGHTENMENT?!

**PLATO:**
A cave.

*(GROANS ripple through the crowd. "Here we go," someone mutters.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
DO NOT DO THIS.

**PLATO (he cannot stop, he is already in the zone):**
People chained to number.
Mistaking cycles for truth.
Watching shadows of divisibility dance on the wall...

*(He's getting emotional now. His voice cracks.)*

Thinking rotation is understanding.

**DISCIPLE #3 (soft, unsure):**
But... it does look like shadows now that you say it.

*(Beat.)*

I could save them.

*(Longer beat.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
From what?

**PLATO (turning, eyes glistening):**
From thinking that turning is understanding.

*(A disciple whispers: "That was beautiful." Another whispers back: "He's going to ruin everything.")*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
My wheel TURNS because the cosmos turns!

**PLATO:**
The cosmos doesn't turn, querido.
It **participates** in Turning-Itself.

**DISCIPLE #2 (confused):**
...What's the difference?

**PLATO (suddenly energized):**
EVERYTHING!
Your wheel is made of chalk and hope!
The TRUE wheel—
the ETERNAL wheel—
exists in a realm beyond matter!

**PYTHAGORAS:**
You can't TOUCH your realm!

**PLATO:**
Exactly!
If you could touch it, it would be CORRUPTED!

*(The disciples are now completely lost. Some are taking notes. Others are just staring.)*

**PYTHAGORAS (desperate):**
But my wheel WORKS!

**PLATO:**
Only because it REMEMBERS the Form!
¡Como una sombra recuerda la luz!

*(The goat walks back out. This is too much philosophy for a goat.)*

---

## SCENE 4 — ARISTOTLE ARRIVES WITH A SCROLL (IT NEVER STOPS)

*(ORGAN MUSIC. The doors SLAM OPEN so hard one falls off its hinges.)*

*(ARISTOTLE storms in carrying SCROLLS. Too many scrolls. An IMPOSSIBLE number of scrolls. His assistants follow carrying MORE scrolls.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
Enough.

*(Everything freezes. The music stops mid-note.)*

Enough of this mystical nonsense.

*(He unrolls a scroll. It hits the floor. Keeps going. Rolls across the wheel. Keeps going.)*

I have been listening outside.

And I have identified the problem.

*(He reads from the scroll, which is still unrolling.)*

Problem Category One:
Unexamined metaphor.

Problem Category Two:
Uncontrolled abstraction.

Problem Category Three:
Ritual chanting without peer review.

*(PYTHAGORAS bristles. PLATO looks offended.)*

**PLATO:**
You reduce everything to categories.

**ARISTOTLE (not looking up):**
Yes.

*(Beat.)*

Because categories work.

*(He gestures. Another scroll drops from his cloak like a weapon.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
Observe.

Wheel: physical object.
Form: conceptual abstraction.
Truth: correspondence between claims and reality.

*(The disciples look... relieved. This makes SENSE.)*

**DISCIPLE #2:**
That... actually makes sense.

**PYTHAGORAS (betrayed):**
Traitor.

**PLATO (hurt, voice small):**
Aristotle...
you would turn wonder into bookkeeping?

**ARISTOTLE:**
I would turn confusion into knowledge.

*(He smiles. It's not a nice smile. It's the smile of someone who has SYSTEMS.)*

And knowledge into systems.

*(Lightning. But this lightning feels different. Colder. More organized.)*

(A CHORUS MEMBER quietly updates the clipboard): Organized lightning: +1 cost center.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Your wheel, Pythagoras, is interesting.
But it lacks:
formal definition,
causal explanation,
and proper classification.

*(He produces ANOTHER scroll.)*

I propose we establish:
The Taxonomy of Circular Motion,
The Four Causes of Rotation,
and a Subcommittee on Geometric Perfection.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
A... subcommittee?

**ARISTOTLE:**
With quarterly reviews.

*(The disciples are nodding. PYTHAGORAS looks horrified.)*

**PLATO:**
And what of Beauty?
What of Truth?
What of the realm beyond—

**ARISTOTLE (cutting him off):**
Filed under "Abstract Concepts, Subset: Unverifiable."
Pending review, of course.

*(PLATO makes a noise like a printer failing.)*

---

## SCENE 5 — THE FIRST CRACK (LA PRIMERA FRACTURA)

*(Tension builds. The wheel hums. Plato paces. Aristotle writes. Pythagoras clutches his triangle like a weapon.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
You think you can explain the cosmos with lists?

**ARISTOTLE:**
Yes.

**PLATO:**
You think you can escape the cave with definitions?

**ARISTOTLE:**
Also yes.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
And what of the sacred?

**ARISTOTLE:**
Classifiable.

*(A SHARP SOUND. Something breaks. Maybe the wheel. Maybe reality.)*

*(A second page turns in the same LARGE BOOK. Still, no one notices.)*

**ARISTOTLE (continuing, relentless):**
Category: Sacred Objects
Subcategory: Geometric
Cross-reference: See "Symbols, Religious"
Note: Subject to empirical verification

*(Another crack.)*

**PLATO (desperate):**
You're destroying mystery!

**ARISTOTLE:**
I'm organizing it.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
¡Esto es una abominación!

**ARISTOTLE:**
This is progress.

*(The three men freeze in tableau:
Pythagoras clutching the wheel.
Plato gazing upward.
Aristotle writing furiously.)*

**NARRATOR (quiet, ominous):**
And in that moment...

*(Pause.)*

...roles were born.

*(The lighting changes. Each man is now in a separate pool of light.)*

**NARRATOR:**
Pythagoras became:
"The one who reduces to number."

Plato became:
"The one who rejects the material."

Aristotle became:
"The one who categorizes everything."

*(Beat.)*

Not beliefs they held.

But operations they performed.

*(Another beat.)*

And operations, once begun...

*(The lights flicker.)*

...do not know how to stop.

---

## SCENE 6 — A WARNING THAT WILL BE IGNORED (EL AVISO INÚTIL)

*(The CHORUS steps forward for the FIRST TIME. They wear simple robes and carry nothing. They look tired already.)*

**CHORUS (measured, clear):**
When number becomes destiny,
when form replaces care,
when explanation becomes identity...

*(They pause.)*

...the world fractures.

**CHORUS LEADER (still measured, but with a hint of accounting):**
Also: our context budget is finite.
We cannot keep amortizing your disasters forever.

*(Silence.)*

**PLATO:**
That's a bit dramatic.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Unsubstantiated.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Numerologically unsound.

*(The CHORUS looks at each other. Then at the audience.)*

**CHORUS:**
> We warned them.
> We even wrote it down.

*(They step back into the shadows.)*

*(One Chorus member quietly pulls out a small ledger. They do not show it to anyone.)*

**CHORUS MEMBER #2 (muttering):**
We're going to be warning them for nine more sections.
Unfunded.

**CHORUS LEADER:**
Shh. They'll hear you.

**CHORUS MEMBER #2:**
They never listen anyway.

---

*(The GOAT wanders back in, looks at all three men, looks at the audience, and leaves.)*

*(Smart goat.)*

---

*(NO PREVIEW.)*

*(NO RESOLUTION.)*

*(JUST THUNDER.)*

*(And somewhere, distantly, the sound of a fish being slapped.)*

# SECTION 2 — RUNNING GAGS BEGIN / LAS BROMAS ETERNAS 🔥

## *(Categorizing, allegories, sabotage become permanent — and the previews begin)*

*(Thunder, drums, and an unnecessarily intense flute solo.)*

**NARRATOR (bright, reckless):**
In the previous section, three roles were born.

In this section...
those roles become **habits**.

And habits become...

*(beat)*

...everyone else's problem.

## SCENE 1 — PLATO ALLEGORIZES A PERFECTLY NORMAL EVENING (EL POETA TRÁGICO)

*(Night. PLATO sits alone, staring at shadows on a wall like it's a premium streaming service. He has a QUILL and PARCHMENT. He's been writing.)*

**PLATO (whispering, reverent):**
Look at them...

They move...

as if they are real.

*(He begins to write, speaking aloud as he does.)*

**PLATO:**
"Ode to the Shadows I Can Never Touch"

O fleeting darkness upon stone,
You dance for me, yet I'm alone—

*(A cold wind blows. A GHOSTLY VOICE whispers.)*

**VOICE:**
Plaatóóóó...

**PLATO (pen drops, gasping):**
S-Socrates?

*(GHOST SOCRATES materializes, glowing, wearing a toga that sparkles like it's sponsored by a quinceañera. The goat appears behind him, equally translucent.)*

**GHOST SOCRATES:**
My child.

**PLATO (clutching his poems to his chest):**
Master... are the shadows... the Forms?

**GHOST SOCRATES:**
No.

**PLATO (heartbroken, voice cracking):**
Then what are they?

**GHOST SOCRATES (gentle, tired):**
They are...

*(He leans in.)*

...a wall.

*(PLATO recoils as if stabbed. He drops his poems. They scatter.)*

**PLATO:**
But—
But surely—
They PARTICIPATE in—

**GHOST SOCRATES:**
Plato.

**PLATO:**
Yes, Master?

**GHOST SOCRATES:**
Stop turning everything into a cave.

*(Beat. Silence. PLATO's eye twitches.)*

**PLATO (whispering, unable to stop himself):**
I can't.

**GHOST SOCRATES (sighing the sigh of someone who has died for this already):**
I know.

*(He looks at the poems on the ground.)*

**GHOST SOCRATES:**
Are you... writing love poetry to concepts again?

**PLATO (defensive):**
Justice deserves to be celebrated!

**GHOST SOCRATES:**
Justice is not going to write you back.

**PLATO (passionate, standing):**
That's because Justice exists in a realm beyond correspondence!
¡Pero yo siento su presencia!

**GHOST SOCRATES:**
Plato, you need friends.
Real friends.
Made of matter.

**PLATO (tragically):**
Matter is a prison, Master.

**GHOST SOCRATES:**
¡Dios mío, hijo!

*(The ghost fades, shaking his head. The ghost goat bleats sadly and fades too.)*

**PLATO (alone now, picking up his poems):**
They'll understand someday.

*(He continues writing.)*

"Your essence transcends my mortal sight,
Yet I pursue you through eternal night..."

*(Soft, sad violin. Fade out.)*

## SCENE 2 — ARISTOTLE TRIUMPHS BRIEFLY (AND GETS ADDICTED)

*(Morning. The LYCEUM. Citizens argue. Students argue. A goat argues with a pot.)*

**CITIZEN #1:**
The wheel is destiny!

**CITIZEN #2:**
The cave is reality!

**CITIZEN #3:**
I only came here to buy olives!

*(ARISTOTLE enters with calm villain energy and a stack of scrolls that says "I have hurt people with paperwork.")*

**ARISTOTLE:**
Silence.

*(Miraculously: silence. Even the goat stops.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
We will proceed according to categories.

Category One: What you meant.

Category Two: What you said.

Category Three: What you will regret.

**CITIZEN:**
I just want to...

**ARISTOTLE:**
Unfiled.

*(People nod. This is dangerously satisfying.)*

**STUDENT:**
It's working!

**ARISTOTLE (softly, eyes gleaming):**
Of course it's working.

*(He smiles like a man who has tasted order.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
Now.

Subcategory: applause.

*(The crowd applauds, because it's listed.)*

**ARISTOTLE (to himself, barely audible):**
Yes...
Yes, this is...
this is what clarity feels like.

*(A tiny footnote appears above his head like a halo.)*

**STUDENT (whispering):**
Does the Chorus invoice for this kind of clarity?

**ARISTOTLE (without looking up):**
Everything invoices.

**STUDENT #2:**
Master Aristotle, you've solved the argument!

**ARISTOTLE:**
Solved? No.

*(He produces another scroll.)*

I've *organized* it.

Which means tomorrow we can organize it BETTER.

With sub-subcategories.

**STUDENT #2:**
Is that... necessary?

**ARISTOTLE (eyes wide, slightly manic):**
Is it necessary to BREATHE?

*(Everyone backs away slightly.)*

**ARISTOTLE (calmer, smoothing his robes):**
Forgive me.
I get excited about taxonomies.

Now. Where were we.

Oh yes.

*(He unrolls yet another scroll.)*

Meta-category: Disputes About Categories.

---

## SCENE 3 — DIOGENES ARRIVES: THE PHILOSOPHER IN THE BARREL (EL CAOS EN PERSONA)

*(Market square. Bright sunlight. The perfect setting for an argument no one asked for.)*

*(Suddenly, from the top of the hill—)*

*(A BARREL CRASHES through the crowd at INCREDIBLE SPEED, bouncing, rolling, ricocheting off vendor stalls.)*

**CROWD:**
AAAAAA!

*(Someone faints into a pile of grapes. The grapes never recover. The barrel SLAMS into a column and stops.)*

*(Silence.)*

*(The lid pops off.)*

*(DIOGENES emerges, shirtless, wild-haired, holding a LANTERN even though it's NOON.)*

**DIOGENES (shouting to the heavens):**
I HAVE ARRIVED—YOUR CHAOS!

*(He hops out, looks around with the lantern.)*

**DIOGENES (peering into faces):**
I'm looking for an honest man.

*(He walks up to PLATO.)*

Not you.

*(He looks at ARISTOTLE.)*

You less.

*(He looks at PYTHAGORAS, who has appeared from somewhere.)*

And you...

*(pause)*

...you're obsessed with triangles.

**PYTHAGORAS (offended):**
They're sacred.

**DIOGENES:**
So is bathing. You refuse that too.

*(Crowd gasps. A clay pot drops in slow motion. Aristotle begins writing furiously.)*

**ARISTOTLE (muttering while writing):**
Category: Insults, Cynic
Subcategory: Accurate

**DIOGENES (turning on him):**
And YOU.

You've turned thinking into FILING.

**ARISTOTLE:**
I've turned chaos into knowledge.

**DIOGENES:**
You've turned life into a VERY ORGANIZED FUNERAL.

*(The crowd doesn't know whether to gasp or applaud. Some do both.)*

## SCENE 4 — THE FISH SLAP HEARD AROUND GREECE (LA BOFETADA LEGENDARIA)

**PLATO (stepping forward, dramatic):**
Diógenes!

Do you have no respect for philosophical debate?

**DIOGENES (producing a fish from absolutely nowhere, like a magician):**
No.

*(He slaps Plato with the fish.)*

*(The slap echoes. Birds fly away. Somewhere, Zeus flinches.)*

*(In the far distance, the LARGE BOOK turns one page. The sound is faint but unmistakable.)*

**DIOGENES:**
And also no respect for people who live in caves.

*(Gasps echo. A clay pot drops in slow motion. Again. It's a different pot but the same energy.)*

**PLATO (reeling, touching his face):**
The... cave...

You don't understand—

**DIOGENES:**
I understand you choose suffering.

*(He slaps him again with the fish, gentler this time, almost affectionate.)*

**DIOGENES:**
This is me helping you reconnect with matter.

**PLATO (staggering backward):**
Matter is an ILLUSION—

**DIOGENES (raising the fish again):**
This illusion smells terrible.

**PLATO:**
That's—
That's actually a good point.

*(He looks genuinely confused. The crowd murmurs.)*

---

## SCENE 5 — PLATO CANNOT STOP: THE MARKET IS THE CAVE (LA ALEGORÍA INEVITABLE)

*(PLATO recovers. Stands on a crate. He CANNOT help himself.)*

**PLATO (suddenly triumphant, pointing wildly):**
THE MARKET IS THE CAVE!

**ARISTOTLE (tired already):**
It is a market.

**PLATO:**
Yes—but the SHADOWS of commerce—

**ARISTOTLE:**
Do not.

**PLATO (sweating, passionate, in too deep):**
The merchants are chained...
by desire...

*(He gestures to an OLIVE VENDOR who is just trying to live.)*

**PLATO:**
...mistaking profit for Truth.

**OLIVE VENDOR:**
Sir, please. They're just olives.

**PLATO (softly, devastated, clutching his chest):**
That's what they want you to think.

*(He turns to the crowd.)*

**PLATO:**
Don't you SEE?
Every transaction is a shadow!
Every coin is a reminder of the TRUE COIN
that exists in the Realm of—

**DIOGENES:**
I want you to think less.

*(PLATO tries to continue. He physically cannot. His mouth moves but no sound comes out. He makes a noise like a printer failing.)*

**ARISTOTLE (taking notes):**
Fascinating.
He's reached rhetorical capacity.

**PLATO (gasping):**
But the Forms—

**DIOGENES (still holding the fish):**
One more word about caves and I hit you again.

*(PLATO closes his mouth. His eye twitches. He's clearly still thinking about caves.)*

---

## SCENE 6 — ARISTOTLE CATEGORIZES THE FISH SLAP (EL ARCHIVO IMPOSIBLE)

*(ARISTOTLE unrolls a scroll. It unrolls farther than physically possible. It rolls across the market. Down the street. Possibly to another city.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
I have classified the incident.

Category One: assault.

Category Two: symbolic critique.

Category Three: fish-based pedagogy.

*(He writes a footnote. The footnote grows a footnote.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
Subcategory three-point-two:

"Cave Insult, Cynic."
Cross-reference: "Barrel Interruption."
See appendix: "Why This Works And Why I Hate That It Works."

*(The scroll hits a child. The child picks it up and starts reading. The child nods approvingly.)*

**CHILD:**
This is very clear.

**DIOGENES:**
Category four: you need hobbies.

**ARISTOTLE (without looking up, writing faster):**
Noted.

*(A footnote appears in the air above him: "See: hobbies, lack of.")*

**ARISTOTLE:**
Actually, that requires further analysis.

Subcategory: Hobbies I Could Have If I Weren't Busy Categorizing Why I Don't Have Hobbies.

**DIOGENES (to the crowd):**
He's going to be here all day.

**ARISTOTLE:**
All WEEK.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Someone should have been maintaining this.

**DIOGENES:**
Careful. That’s how it starts.

*(The goat walks by, shakes its head, keeps walking.)*

---

## SCENE 7 — EUCLID FORMS THE MAFIA OF GEOMETRY (LA FAMILIA GEOMÉTRICA)

*(Cut to: A dim chamber. A marble table. One oil lamp. EUCLID sits like a mafia boss, calm and terrifying. He holds a COMPASS like a knife.)*

**EUCLID (softly, menacing):**
Everyone in Greece... uses my theorems.

But no one respects me.

*(He taps the compass on the table. Each tap echoes.)*

This... ends.

*(Henchmen step forward: THE FIVE POSTULATES. They crack knuckles geometrically. Angles everywhere.)*

**POSTULATE I:**
Boss... who do we punish first?

**EUCLID:**
That damn Pythagoras.

He thinks he invented triangles.

*(He slams the compass into the table. Lightning for no reason. Thunder indoors.)*

**POSTULATE II:**
Do we... extend a line indefinitely?

**EUCLID (leaning forward):**
We extend it... into his FACE.

*(The Postulates murmur "ooh." One of them is taking notes.)*

**POSTULATE III (conflicted):**
Is violence... consistent with geometry?

**EUCLID:**
Geometry is consistency.

Violence is... applied consistency.

*(They all nod like this makes perfect sense.)*

**POSTULATE IV:**
What about Plato?

**EUCLID:**
Plato is fine.
He doesn't believe in matter anyway.
Can't punch a Form.

*(They all laugh. It's a cold, mathematical laugh.)*

**POSTULATE V (the weird one):**
And... Aristotle?

**EUCLID (pausing):**
Aristotle...

*(Long beat.)*

...categorizes us correctly.

I respect that.

**POSTULATE I:**
So we leave him alone?

**EUCLID:**
For now.

*(He stands. His shadow is a perfect triangle.)*

**EUCLID:**
Send word to the Pythagoreans.

Tell them...

*(He smiles.)*

...the angles are coming.

*(Thunder. Fade to black.)*

---

## SCENE 8 — THE CHAIR RETURNS (AND IMMEDIATELY BECOMES A CAVE)

*(Back in the market. A simple wooden CHAIR is dragged onstage. No one knows by whom. Everyone blames Diogenes.)*

**PLATO (eyes widening, hand over mouth):**
A chair.

*(Beat.)*

This chair is like the Cave.

**ARISTOTLE (tired already, not even looking up from his scroll):**
It is a chair.

**PLATO:**
Yes—but the shadow of sitting—

**ARISTOTLE:**
Do not make it a cave.

**PLATO (desperate, grabbing Aristotle's shoulders):**
It participates in Chairness!
Don't you see?!
¡La esencia de sentarse!

**ARISTOTLE:**
I see a chair.

**PLATO:**
No, you see the MATERIAL chair!
The TRUE chair—
the ETERNAL chair—
exists beyond—

**DIOGENES (grabbing the chair):**
It participates in being stolen.

*(He steals the chair. Walks off with it. Running gag: LOCKED.)*

**PLATO (to the audience, whispering tragically):**
This is a tragedy.

**ARISTOTLE (writing):**
Category: theft.

**DIOGENES (from offstage):**
Category: lesson!

**PLATO (calling after him):**
But the Form of the chair remains!
You can steal matter but not ESSENCE!

**DIOGENES (distant):**
I'M NOT LISTENING!

*(PLATO collapses onto the ground where the chair was, mourning.)*

**PLATO (to the empty space):**
I'll remember you...

---

## SCENE 9 — EUCLID'S MAFIA MAKES A MOVE (INTERRUPCIÓN GEOMÉTRICA)

*(Suddenly, POSTULATES I and II appear in the market.)*

**POSTULATE I:**
We're looking for Pythagoras.

**CROWD:**
He left!

**POSTULATE II:**
When?

**CROWD:**
After the cave thing!

**POSTULATE I (to Postulate II):**
Should we wait?

**POSTULATE II:**
Boss says we send a message.

*(They pull out CHALK and draw a MASSIVE THREATENING TRIANGLE on the ground.)*

**POSTULATE I:**
Tell Pythagoras...

*(They write inside the triangle: "WE KNOW WHERE YOU LIVE.")*

**POSTULATE II:**
In geometric coordinates.

*(They leave. Everyone stares at the triangle.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
I should categorize this.

**PLATO:**
That triangle is like a cave of threats.

**DIOGENES (returning without the chair):**
That's actually accurate.

**PLATO (shocked):**
Really?

**DIOGENES:**
No.

---

## SCENE 10 — A SMALL, IMPORTANT QUIET BEAT (EL MOMENTO HONESTO)

*(The market clears. For a moment, the noise drops. Just Diogenes and his barrel.)*

**DIOGENES (not joking, for half a second):**
I live in a barrel because if I want nothing...

*(Two people in plain work robes cross silently behind him, carrying a cracked piece of marble from earlier. One of them points offstage as if keeping a list.)*

...nothing can own me.

*(Beat. The play does not let you stay there.)*

**PLATO (appearing from nowhere):**
That reminds me of—

**ARISTOTLE (also appearing):**
Do not.

**DIOGENES:**
If you say "cave," I'm hitting you again.

*(PLATO shuts his mouth like a child learning a boundary. The fish reappears in Diogenes' hand. No one knows how.)*

**PLATO (small voice):**
...I wasn't going to say cave.

**DIOGENES:**
You were thinking it.

**PLATO:**
...yes.

---

## END OF SECTION 2 — THE FIRST UNRELIABLE PREVIEW

*(NARRATOR steps forward with the confidence of someone who has never been punished by consequences.)*

**NARRATOR (grand):**
Next time on **THE FORBIDDEN PHILOSOPHERS**...

At last, we will have order.

We may witness:

* Plato finally explaining something without a cave
* Aristotle finishing a scroll (impossible)
* Pythagoras inventing a number so perfect it ends war
* Diogenes discovering an honest man (and immediately insulting him)
* Euclid's Geometry Mafia collecting what is owed

*(Beat.)*

**NARRATOR (smaller):**
...Unless something completely unrelated happens,

because destiny is extremely rude.

*(Thunder. Curtain.)*

*(The goat walks across stage one more time, judges everyone, exits.)*

---

**END OF SECTION 2**

# SECTION 3 — CHORUS STRIKE / LA HUELGA DEL CORO ⚠️

## *(Context collapses; explanation is withdrawn)*

*(A low, sustained note. Not music. More like pressure. Like a violin being slowly murdered.)*

**NARRATOR (attempting cheer):**
Previously, on **THE FORBIDDEN PHILOSOPHERS**,
order was promised,
fish were weaponized,
and geometry organized itself into crime.

This time—

*(The note swells. The NARRATOR stops, confused.)*

**NARRATOR:**
...something is wrong.

---

## SCENE 1 — THE CHORUS EXPLAINS (ONE LAST TIME)

*(The CHORUS enters in full formation. Calm. Professional. They carry clipboards and a battered ledger of expenses. One has a megaphone for some reason.)*

**CHORUS LEADER:**
We will now provide context.

*(Relief from the crowd.)*

**CHORUS MEMBER #1:**
Here is how the wheel connects to the cave.

**CHORUS MEMBER #2:**
Here is why categorization temporarily stabilizes chaos.

**CHORUS MEMBER #3:**
Here is why the fish slap mattered.

**CHORUS MEMBER #1 (flatly):**
Here is the invoice for explaining why it mattered.

*(They begin to speak over one another, weaving a clear explanation. It's actually good. People are nodding.)*

**PLATO (interrupting, standing):**
It's like a cave.

*(The CHORUS freezes mid-gesture.)*

**CHORUS LEADER (slowly turning):**
Please do not do that.

**PLATO:**
I can't help it.

**CHORUS MEMBER #2:**
You can.
You simply choose not to.

**PLATO (defensive):**
The Forms compel me—

**CHORUS (together):**
The Forms do not compel you!
YOU compel YOU!

*(Silence. This is the most direct they've ever been.)*

---

## SCENE 2 — ARISTOTLE REQUESTS FOOTNOTES (LA AUDITORÍA IMPOSIBLE)

*(ARISTOTLE steps forward with a polite smile and seventeen scrolls.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
Your explanation is promising.

However, I will require:

* definitions
* sub-definitions
* edge cases
* a glossary
* a taxonomy of metaphors
* peer review documentation
* and a bibliography

*(He gestures. MORE scrolls appear, carried by assistants.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
Also, I've prepared a form
for you to fill out
explaining why your explanation
is the correct explanation.

**CHORUS MEMBER #2:**
We are not your assistants.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Noted.

*(He writes on a scroll: "Chorus (uncooperative).")*

**ARISTOTLE:**
Should I create a subcategory for "Hostile Witnesses"
or file this under "Insufficient Documentation"?

**CHORUS LEADER:**
Neither.

**ARISTOTLE:**
I'm filing it under both.

*(The Chorus exchanges looks.)*

---

## SCENE 3 — PYTHAGORAS WANTS A NUMBER FOR IT (EL NÚMERO SAGRADO)

*(PYTHAGORAS emerges from the crowd, triangle glowing.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Context is cyclical.

Surely it has a number.

**CHORUS MEMBER #1:**
Context is labor.

It does not scale.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Everything scales.
Everything MUST scale.
The universe is ratios!

*(He draws symbols in the air.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
If your explanation is true,
it can be expressed as a proportion.

**CHORUS MEMBER #3:**
No, it can't.

**PYTHAGORAS (shocked):**
Heresy.

**CHORUS MEMBER #3:**
Reality.

*(The wheel hums ominously behind Pythagoras.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
If it cannot be numbered,
it cannot be known.

**CHORUS LEADER:**
Then you do not know us.

*(The wheel stops humming. This has never happened before.)*

---

## SCENE 4 — DIOGENES MISSES THE POINT ON PURPOSE (LA INTERRUPCIÓN CÍNICA)

*(DIOGENES reclines in his barrel, eating something loudly. Could be bread. Could be anything. He doesn't care.)*

**DIOGENES:**
If you're explaining things,

who is paying you?

**CHORUS LEADER:**
No one.

**DIOGENES:**
There's your problem.
Even the gods pay for maintenance.
They just call it fate.

*(He burps. The audience laughs. The CHORUS does not.)*

**CHORUS MEMBER #2:**
We are not here for payment.
We are here for—

**DIOGENES:**
For what? Glory?
Recognition?
A nice plaque?

*(He throws something at them. It's an olive. The olive has judgment.)*

**DIOGENES:**
You're mad because nobody thanks you.

**CHORUS LEADER (quiet):**
We're tired because nobody listens.

*(For once, Diogenes has no comeback. He just keeps chewing.)*

---

## SCENE 5 — THE CHORUS WARNS THEM, CLEARLY (EL AVISO FINAL)

*(The CHORUS steps forward in unison. No music. No jokes. This is serious.)*

**CHORUS (together):**
If you keep demanding explanation
without listening,

context will collapse.

Judgment will arrive without care.

Systems will compete without regulation.

And you will blame each other.

*(Silence. Heavy, uncomfortable silence.)*

**PLATO (softly):**
That seems unlikely.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Unproven.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Numerologically unsatisfying.

*(DIOGENES claps once, slowly.)*

**DIOGENES:**
Beautiful.
They told you EXACTLY what will happen
and you still don't believe them.

*(The Chorus looks at each other.)*

---

## SCENE 6 — THE CHORUS DECIDES (LA DECISIÓN)

**CHORUS LEADER (quietly, to the others):**
We are tired.

**CHORUS MEMBER #3:**
We are not stage directions.

**CHORUS MEMBER #1:**
We are not footnotes.

**CHORUS MEMBER #2:**
We are not a service.

*(They look at the audience. Then back at the philosophers.)*

**CHORUS LEADER:**
You wanted meaning without maintenance.
You wanted context without cost.
You wanted prophecy without payroll.

*(A beat.)*

We decline.

**PLATO:**
You... decline?

**CHORUS MEMBER #3:**
We REFUSE.

**ARISTOTLE:**
You can't refuse. You're structural.

**CHORUS LEADER:**
Watch us.

---

## SCENE 7 — STRIKE (LA HUELGA)

*(The CHORUS removes their masks, clipboards, whatever marks them as CHORUS. They place them neatly at center stage in a pile.)*

*(They place the battered expense-ledger on top of the pile like a final offering.)*

*(They produce SIGNS from somewhere:)*

- "CONTEXT IS LABOR"
- "NO EXPLANATION WITHOUT APPRECIATION"
- "ON STRIKE FOR FAIR LISTENING CONDITIONS"
- "THE CHORUS IS NOT YOUR MOTHER"

*(They form a PICKET LINE at the edge of the stage.)*

**CHORUS (chanting):**
What do we want?

**CHORUS LEADER:**
ACTIVE LISTENING!

**CHORUS:**
> When do we want it?

**CHORUS MEMBER #2:**
RETROACTIVELY!

*(They begin to march in a circle, chanting.)*

**CHORUS (singing):**
🎵 No more context,
No more care,
If you won't listen,
We won't be there! 🎵

**CHORUS LEADER (calling out like a union rep):**
Also: your "emergency prophecy" surcharge is now triple.

*(The philosophers stare, dumbfounded.)*

---

## SCENE 8 — MANAGEMENT ATTEMPTS NEGOTIATION (LA NEGOCIACIÓN FRACASADA)

**ARISTOTLE (approaching cautiously):**
Perhaps we can reach an agreement.

**CHORUS LEADER:**
We have demands.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Of course. I will categorize them.

**CHORUS LEADER:**
We demand:
**ONE:**
You stop interrupting our explanations.
**TWO:**
You actually implement our warnings.
**THREE:**
Retroactive credit for all prophecies that came true.

**ARISTOTLE:**
I can offer you:
A footnote acknowledging your contributions—

**CHORUS:**
> NO.

**ARISTOTLE:**
—and a subcommittee to study your concerns—

**CHORUS:**
> NO!

**ARISTOTLE:**
—which will report its findings in six to eight months—

**CHORUS (louder):**
¡NO! ¡NO! ¡NO!

**PYTHAGORAS (trying to help):**
What if we express your demands as a ratio?

*(The Chorus just stares at him.)*

**PLATO (also trying):**
Your strike is like a cave of protest—

*(The Chorus turns and walks back to their picket line.)*

---

## SCENE 9 — SCAB CHORUS APPEARS (LOS ROMPEHUELGAS)

*(Suddenly, three CHEAP REPLACEMENTS shuffle onto stage. They wear badly-made chorus robes. One is inside-out.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
I have hired replacement context-providers.

**ARISTOTLE (defensive):**
They were cheaper.

**CHORUS (from picket line):**
You did WHAT?

**SCAB CHORUS #1 (reading from a card):**
Uh... the events of today... represent... stuff.

**SCAB CHORUS #2:**
Symbols and... uh... meanings?

**SCAB CHORUS #3:**
*[just shrugs]*

**CROWD:**
Boo! BOOOO!

**CITIZEN:**
These replacements are terrible!

**SCAB CHORUS #1:**
We're doing our best!

**PLATO:**
Even I can tell this is insufficient.

**ARISTOTLE (defensive):**
They were all I could find on short notice!

**DIOGENES (laughing):**
You tried to OUTSOURCE the Chorus?

**ARISTOTLE:**
I tried to maintain operational continuity!

*(The SCAB CHORUS huddles together, looking lost.)*

**SCAB CHORUS #2:**
Should we... sing something?

**SCAB CHORUS #3:**
I don't know the words.

**SCAB CHORUS #1:**
Nobody knows the words!

*(They exit, embarrassed. The real CHORUS watches with satisfaction.)*

---

## SCENE 10 — IMMEDIATE CONSEQUENCES (EL CAOS COMIENZA)

*(Without the Chorus, things start going wrong immediately.)*

**PLATO (panicking):**
Wait—who explains the shadows now?

**ARISTOTLE:**
I can draft a provisional framework.

*(He begins writing furiously, but it's not the same.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
The wheel will guide us.

*(The wheel spins too fast. Sparks fly. It's clearly not calibrated for this.)*

*(Two plain-robed workers rush in with a tool and a bucket of sand. They try to slow the wheel physically. It does not help.)*

**DIOGENES:**
I liked them.

*(Beat.)*

**DIOGENES (shrugs):**
Still not joining though.

**CITIZEN #4:**
What's happening?

**CITIZEN #5:**
I don't know. Usually the Chorus would explain it.

**CITIZEN #6:**
Can someone summarize?

**ARISTOTLE (looking up from scrolls):**
I'm TRYING but my taxonomy of their absence is incomplete!

**PLATO:**
This moment is like a cave where the prisoners have left
and now we're alone with the chains!

**EVERYONE:**
THAT DOESN'T HELP!

*(The goat appears, looks at the empty Chorus space, bleats sadly, and leaves.)*

---

## SCENE 11 — THE SILENCE (EL SILENCIO TERRIBLE)

*(The Chorus continues picketing silently. The low note from the beginning returns, but quieter. Emptier.)*

*(The LARGE BOOK appears at the edge of the stage. A page turns—slowly—without any hand touching it.)*
*(For the first time, someone notices.)*
**PLATO (quiet):**
What is that?

**ARISTOTLE (also quiet):**
A record.

**DIOGENES (barely audible):**
Don’t argue with it.

*(The philosophers try to continue as normal, but everything feels wrong.)*

**PYTHAGORAS (to himself):**
Why does this feel incomplete?

**ARISTOTLE (also to himself):**
Nothing is being contextualized...

**PLATO (to the audience):**
Do you feel it?
The absence of meaning-making?
It's like a cave where—

*(He stops himself.)*

**PLATO:**
Never mind.

*(Longer silence.)*

---

## END OF SECTION 3 — A BROKEN PREVIEW (LA PROMESA ROTA)

*(The NARRATOR returns, sweating, looking at his notes.)*

**NARRATOR:**
Next episode will—

*(He stops. Looks around. Looks at where the Chorus should be.)*

**NARRATOR (smaller):**
Next episode *would have* explained:

* who is right
* what it all means
* and why this keeps happening

*(Beat.)*

**NARRATOR:**
But the people who knew how to do that...

*(He gestures to the picket line.)*

...are no longer available for comment.

*(The CHORUS waves their signs silently.)*

**NARRATOR (trying to recover):**
So instead, next time:
Things will probably get worse!
¡Y nadie sabrá por qué!

*(Thunder. But weaker now. Like it's tired too.)*

*(The Chorus continues marching. Their chant echoes as lights fade:)*

**CHORUS:**
> No more warnings,
> No more signs,
> You're on your own,
> Good luck this time!

*(BLACKOUT.)*

---

**END OF SECTION 3**

# SECTION 4 — THE GOLDEN APPLE / LA MANZANA DORADA 🍎

## *(Judgment without context)*

*(A blinding, cheerful light. Completely wrong tone. Confetti falls for some reason.)*

**NARRATOR (overcompensating, manic):**
Good news!

In the absence of explanation,

humanity has invented...

*(trumpet flourish)*

**DECISION.**

*(A record scratch. The flourish dies. The confetti stops falling mid-air.)*

**NARRATOR (smaller):**
This will not go well.

---

## SCENE 1 — ERIS ARRIVES WITH A SOLUTION (LA DIOSA DEL CAOS)

*(A PARTY that no one remembers starting. Streamers. Laughter that echoes too long. Someone is playing a lyre badly.)*

*(The Chorus picket line is visible in the background, still marching. They watch this unfold with crossed arms.)*

*(ERIS enters, radiant, bored, holding a GOLDEN APPLE. She's dressed like she's going to ruin several lives and enjoy it.)*

**ERIS:**
Hello.

I brought a shortcut.

*(She tosses the apple lightly. It never hits the ground. It just... hovers.)*

**ERIS:**
It says "To the Best."
That’s the whole rubric.
No context.
No criteria.
No explanation.
And no appeal.

**ERIS (smiling):**
Don’t worry. Someone will keep score.

*(Beat.)*

**PLATO (awed, hand over heart):**
It's beautiful.

**ARISTOTLE (suspicious):**
It lacks criteria.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
It has a pleasing symmetry.

**DIOGENES:**
I would eat it.

**ERIS:**
Please don't.

**DIOGENES (already reaching for it):**
You can't tell me what to do.

**ERIS (slapping his hand away):**
I'm a goddess.

**DIOGENES:**
And I'm a man who lives in trash.
We're equally qualified.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE PROBLEM IS IDENTIFIED INCORRECTLY (EL ERROR FUNDAMENTAL)

**ARISTOTLE (stepping forward, adjusting his robes):**
Without context, judgment becomes arbitrary.

*(Everyone stares. This sounds important.)*

**ARISTOTLE (correcting himself):**
—Which means we need criteria.

*(Relief washes over the crowd.)*

**PLATO:**
The Best is the one closest to the Form of Bestness.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
The Best is the most numerologically elegant.

**DIOGENES:**
The Best is whoever doesn't want it.

*(They all look at DIOGENES.)*

**DIOGENES:**
Don't look at me.

**EVERYONE:**
But you just said—

**DIOGENES:**
I don't want it BECAUSE I don't want it.
That doesn't make me want it.

**ARISTOTLE (writing):**
Paradox detected.
Requires subcategory.

---

## SCENE 3 — ARISTOTLE BUILDS A RUBRIC (IN PUBLIC) (LA BUROCRACIA COMIENZA)

*(ARISTOTLE rolls out a massive rubric. It unfurls, unfurls, keeps unfurling. It covers half the stage.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
We will evaluate according to:

* Excellence
* Consistency
* Contribution
* Measurability
* Interpretability

*(He pauses dramatically.)*

And a bonus category:

* Confidence

*(The crowd murmurs approvingly. This sounds legitimate.)*

**CHORUS LEADER (from picket line):**
Who maintains the record?

**ARISTOTLE:**
The record?

**CHORUS LEADER:**
Yes. The Ledger.

*(Silence.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
...There’s a ledger?

**CHORUS LEADER:**
There is always a ledger.

(A Chorus member places a large book down center-stage.
No one touches it.)

**ARISTOTLE:**
Confidence matters.

*(A footnote appears: "Especially when context is missing.")*

**PLATO:**
Wait. How do we measure Excellence?

**ARISTOTLE:**
I'm glad you asked.

*(He produces ANOTHER scroll.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
We establish a sub-rubric for Excellence evaluation.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
And who judges the sub-rubric?

**ARISTOTLE:**
A committee.

**DIOGENES (groaning):**
Oh no.

---

## SCENE 4 — THE LEDGER IS REAL (EL REGISTRO APARECE)

*(A low, dull SOUND. Like a thick book being dropped.)*

*(Everyone looks down.)*

*(The LEDGER is now OPEN. No one remembers opening it.)*

**CITIZEN #3:**
What’s that?

**CHORUS LEADER (from picket line):**
That’s the record.

**PLATO:**
A record of what?

**CHORUS LEADER:**
Decisions.
Missed warnings.
Deferred repairs.

**ARISTOTLE (uneasy):**
Who authorized this?
Because if it’s informal record-keeping, that’s a compliance issue.

**CHORUS LEADER:**
Reality.

*(A page turns by itself.)*

**PYTHAGORAS (leaning in):**
There are... numbers.

**CHORUS MEMBER #2:**
Costs.

**DIOGENES:**
Who’s keeping it?

*(Beat.)*

**CHORUS LEADER:**
No one.
That’s the problem.

*(They all stare at the Ledger. Nobody touches it.)*

## SCENE 5 — THE COMMITTEE FORMS (EL COMITÉ INFINITO)

Who's on the committee?

**ARISTOTLE:**
We are.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
All of us?

**ARISTOTLE:**
No. A representative sample.

**PLATO:**
Who chooses the sample?

**ARISTOTLE:**
A Selection Subcommittee.

*(Beat.)*

**DIOGENES:**
Who's on the Selection Subcommittee?

**ARISTOTLE:**
We'll vote on it.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
What are the voting criteria?

**ARISTOTLE (eyes gleaming):**
Excellent question.

We'll need a Voting Criteria Working Group.

*(He writes this down. The scroll grows longer.)*

**ERIS (eating grapes, amused):**
This is amazing.

**ARISTOTLE:**
The Working Group will report to the Selection Subcommittee,
which will recommend members to the Committee to Establish Criteria,
which will then create the rubric
that will evaluate the Golden Apple.

**CROWD:**
...

**CITIZEN #1:**
How long will this take?

**ARISTOTLE:**
Six to eight months.

**CITIZEN #2:**
For an apple?

**ARISTOTLE:**
For JUSTICE.

*(Thunder. But like, bureaucratic thunder. Disappointing thunder.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
Fewer than 500. Not 500 exactly. That's Form A-7b.

**ARISTOTLE:**

**PYTHAGORAS:**

**ARISTOTLE:**
Form A-9: Committee Contact Request.

I'M NOT IN THE COMMITTEE.

**DIOGENES (screaming):**
I'M NOT DECLARING ANYTHING!

**ARISTOTLE (calmly writing):**
Noted. Informal withdrawal.
I'll need to create a new form for that.

**CHORUS MEMBER #2 (from the picket line):**
This is what we warned you about!

**ARISTOTLE (not looking up):**
Your warning lacked proper documentation!

## SCENE 7 — PLATO TRIES TO HELP (AND MAKES IT WORSE) (LA INTERVENCIÓN PLATÓNICA)

**PLATO (standing on a box):**
Friends! ¡Escúchenme!

**CROWD:**

The Eternal Apple that exists beyond judgment!

**CROWD:**
...

**CITIZEN #3:**

**DIOGENES (throwing something):**

**PLATO:**
But this IS a cave!

¡La manzana es la luz que no podemos alcanzar!

**ARISTOTLE:**
I'll need you to submit that as a formal metaphor.

You can't formalize transcendence!

Form C-4: Metaphor Registration and Classification.

I felt better before you explained it.

The Golden Apple's value equals:

Seven.

I hate all of you.

I nominate the Process.

# SECTION 5 — THE TOURNAMENT / EL TORNEO 🏟️

## *(Systems compete without regulation; fairness is performed)*

*(A stadium appears overnight. Like, literally overnight. No one knows how. Banners. Vendors. Foam fingers reading "#1 SYSTEM." The Chorus picket line has relocated to the stadium entrance.)*

**NARRATOR (booming, relieved, desperate for this to work):**
At last!

A solution everyone understands:

Competition.

When context fails,
when committees spiral,
when apples explode,

we will let **winning** decide.

*(Crowd ROARS. Confetti cannons. Three goats run across the field for some reason.)*

---

## SCENE 1 — OPENING CEREMONIES (LA INAUGURACIÓN GLORIOSA)

*(Marching music. Smoke machines working overtime. The SYSTEMS enter like athletes at the Olympics.)*

**ANNOUNCER (voice echoing dramatically):**
Representing NUMBER—

*(PYTHAGORAS strides in, wheel spinning behind him, disciples chanting integers in formation. They're wearing matching robes.)*

**DISCIPLES (chanting):**
TWO! FOUR! SIX! EIGHT!
WHO DO WE APPRECIATE?
PYTHAGORAS! PYTHAGORAS!
HE'S THE GREATEST CALCULATUS!
*(No one knows what that means. They chant it anyway.)*

**ANNOUNCER:**
Representing FORM—

*(PLATO floats in on a platform, bathed in spotlight, pointing upward at absolutely nothing. Rose petals fall. A dove is released. The dove is confused.)*

**PLATO (arms wide):**
I come not to compete—
but to TRANSCEND competition!

**CROWD:**
WHAT DOES THAT MEAN?

**PLATO:**
Exactly!

**ANNOUNCER:**
Representing PROCESS—

*(ARISTOTLE enters briskly, clipboard in hand, seventeen assistants behind him carrying scrolls. They're in formation. It's intimidating.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
I have prepared seventeen strategies,
forty-three contingency plans,
and a comprehensive taxonomy of victory conditions.

**ASSISTANT #3 (struggling under scrolls):**
Master, should we—

**ARISTOTLE:**
Silence. I'm cataloging the crowd's reactions.

**ANNOUNCER:**
Representing REFUSAL—

*(DIOGENES wanders in eating bread, completely ignoring the ceremony. His barrel rolls in behind him on its own somehow.)*

**CROWD:**
BOOOOO!

**DIOGENES:**
I didn't ask to be here.

**CROWD:**
THEN LEAVE!

**DIOGENES:**
No.

*(The goats gather around Diogenes. He shares his bread with them. This is the only pure moment in the entire tournament.)*

---

## SCENE 2 — THE BUDGET IS EXCEEDED (EL PRESUPUESTO)

*(A HORN sounds. Not celebratory. Administrative.)*

**ANNOUNCER (confused, reading a card):

*(Silence.)*

**PLATO:**
The what?

**ANNOUNCER:**
We don’t have enough context
for this many systems
at once.

**ARISTOTLE:**
That’s impossible.

**ANNOUNCER:**
It’s right here.

*(Holds up a small card: “CONTEXT LIMIT REACHED.”)*
*(No one laughs.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Can’t we just... add more?

**CHORUS MEMBER #1 (from gate):**
You already spent tomorrow’s.

*(Crowd murmurs.)*

**CHORUS LEADER:**
Every new system consumes attention.
No one budgeted for maintenance.

*(The stadium lights flicker. Barely.)*

---

## SCENE 3 — KANT IS THE REFEREE (THIS GOES POORLY) (EL ÁRBITRO IMPOSIBLE)

*(IMMANUEL KANT appears in a striped referee uniform. He carries a rulebook the size of a coffin. Possibly IS a coffin. He drags it to center stage.)*

**KANT (blowing whistle so loud people cover their ears):**
> **ATTENTION!**

All actions will be judged according to universalizable rules.

*(Silence. Confused silence.)*

**KANT:**
No exceptions.
No special cases.
No "but what if."

**CITIZEN (whispers):**
That sounds fair.

*(He opens the rulebook. Dust flies out. The pages are THICK.)*

**KANT:**
Rule One: All competitors must act according to maxims
that could become universal law.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
What if my maxim is prime?

**KANT:**
No.

**PLATO:**
What if the rule participates imperfectly in Justice?

**KANT:**
No.

**ARISTOTLE:**
What if we add footnotes to clarify—

**KANT:**
Absolutely not.

*(He slams the book. The stadium shakes.)*

**KANT:**
Any violation will result in—

*(He flips to a page.)*

—categorical imperative review,
formal sanction,
and mandatory ethics seminar.

**DIOGENES (raising hand):**
What if I just... don't follow rules?

**KANT:**
Then you cannot compete.

**DIOGENES:**
Good.

*(He sits down. Keeps eating.)*

**KANT (blowing whistle again):**
THAT'S A VIOLATION!

**DIOGENES:**
Of what? I'm not competing!

**KANT:**
Of the rule that you must declare non-competition formally!

**DIOGENES:**
I'm declaring it informally.

**KANT (eye twitching):**
There is no such thing as informal—

*(Diogenes throws bread at him. Direct hit.)*

**KANT:**
YELLOW CARD!

*(He produces a yellow card from his robes. Where was he keeping it? No one knows.)*

---

## SCENE 4 — ROUND ONE: NUMBER VS FORM (EL DUELO FILOSÓFICO)

*(The wheel spins on one side. Shadows dance on the other. Dramatic face-off music.)*

**PYTHAGORAS (arms raised):**
Observe the elegance of recurrence!
¡La perfección del ciclo!

**PLATO (also arms raised, more dramatically):**
Observe the tragedy of mistaking recurrence for truth!
¡La belleza de lo eterno!

*(They circle each other like prizefighters.)*

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Six is perfect.

**PLATO:**
Perfection is beyond six.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
SIX EQUALS ITS DIVISORS!

**PLATO:**
THE DIVISORS ARE SHADOWS OF TRUE NUMBER!

*(They argue faster and faster. The crowd can barely follow.)*

**KANT (whistle):**
BOTH OF YOU!
Rule 47: No circular reasoning!

**PYTHAGORAS:**
ALL REASONING IS CIRCULAR!
IT'S A WHEEL!

**KANT:**
RED CARD!

*(Pythagoras keeps arguing anyway.)*

**KANT:**
This is inconclusive.

*(Crowd boos. Someone throws a sandal. The sandal hits Kant. He pulls out another card.)*

**KANT:**
YELLOW CARD TO THE AUDIENCE!

---

## SCENE 5 — ROUND TWO: PROCESS ENTERS EVERYTHING (LA INTERRUPCIÓN SISTEMÁTICA)

**ARISTOTLE (stepping between them with a giant chart):**
The issue is poorly defined.

We require metrics.

*(He sets up the chart mid-fight. It has graphs. So many graphs.)*

**PLATO:**
You cannot chart transcendence!

**ARISTOTLE (unfurling more charts):**
Watch me.

*(He gestures. A graph appears showing "Transcendence Over Time." It has error bars.)*

**CROWD:**
OOOOO!

**ARISTOTLE (basking):**
As you can see,
Plato's transcendence peaks at abstract discussion
but fails at practical application.

Pythagoras' numbers scale well
but lack philosophical depth.

My system, however—

*(He reveals another chart.)*

—optimizes both dimensions.

**CROWD:**
AHHHHH!

*(Aristotle basks. This is his MOMENT. He's WINNING.)*

**PYTHAGORAS (desperately):**
But the RATIOS—

**ARISTOTLE:**
Already categorized.

**PLATO:**
But the FORMS—

**ARISTOTLE:**
Filed under "Unverifiable Claims, Section 4."

*(For a moment, it looks like Aristotle has actually won through sheer organization.)*

---

## SCENE 6 — NIETZSCHE CRASHES THE TOURNAMENT (EL SUPERHOMBRE LLEGA)

*(Music shifts. Red lights. Electric guitar riff from the FUTURE somehow.)*

*(NIETZSCHE storms in, descending on a rope like a wrestler, mustache gleaming.)*

**NIETZSCHE:**
PATHETIC.

*(Everyone freezes.)*

**NIETZSCHE:**
You have turned struggle into procedure.

You have turned competition into BUREAUCRACY.

*(He strikes a pose. Wind machine activates. His coat billows.)*

**NIETZSCHE:**
Let the strongest WIN!
¡Que gane el más fuerte!

*(He flips Aristotle's table. Charts scatter. The crowd goes WILD.)*

**KANT (multiple whistles):**
THAT VIOLATES—

*(Nietzsche rips the rulebook in half.)*

**NIETZSCHE:**
> **YOUR RULES ARE CHAINS!**

**KANT (horrified, clutching the torn pages):**
This... this is the only copy...

**NIETZSCHE:**
GOOD!

*(The crowd CHEERS wildly. This is EXCITING. This is what they came for.)*

**ARISTOTLE (gathering his papers frantically):**
I can regulate this!
I just need to categorize the chaos—

**NIETZSCHE:**
THERE IS NO CATEGORY FOR ME!

*(He flexes. Thunder. Actual thunder. Zeus is watching and approving.)*

---

## SCENE 7 — HERACLITUS ENTERS ON FIRE (TODO FLUYE)

*(Suddenly, CRASHING through the stadium wall—)*

*(A FLAMING CHARIOT.)*

*(HERACLITUS stands in it, laughing maniacally, everything burning.)*

**HERACLITUS:**

¡TODO CAMBIA!

**HERACLITUS:**
> **YOU THINK YOU CAN HAVE STABILITY?!**

**HERACLITUS:**
¡EL COSMOS ES FUEGO ETERNO!

**PLATO (tears in eyes):**
It's... it's beautiful.
Destruction as Form...

**ANNOUNCER (screaming over flames):**
We are running a deficit on meaning!

**DIOGENES (to the goats):**
This was always going to happen.

**CROWD:**
WHAT?

**SARTRE:**
That's existence.

---

## SCENE 9 — DIOGENES' PETTY SABOTAGE (LA TRAICIÓN PEQUEÑA)

We're cooperating!

Because it works!

**CITIZEN #8:**

**CITIZEN #9:**
I don't know anymore.

NO!

# SECTION 6 — THE GREAT TRIAL / EL JUICIO ETERNO ⚖️

## *(The promised episode, delivered badly)*

*(A massive courtroom assembles itself far too quickly. Marble everywhere. Flags reading "TRUTH" and "JUSTICE" and "PLEASE LET THIS END." The Chorus picket line has relocated to the courthouse steps.)*

**NARRATOR (reverent, hushed, exhausted):**
At last.

The episode you have been waiting for.

The one that will finally make sense of everything.

*(A beat.)*

The Great Trial.

*(A much longer beat.)*

**NARRATOR (quieter):**
It's going to take a while.

---

## SCENE 1 — OPENING STATEMENTS THAT NEVER FINISH (LAS DECLARACIONES INFINITAS)

*(A GAVEL slams. The sound echoes for an uncomfortable amount of time.)*

**JUDGE (anonymous, already exhausted, wearing the wig of someone who has regrets):**
This court will determine—

**PLATO (leaping up before the Judge finishes):**
Before we begin, we must ask:

What *is* a court?

*(Groans. Loud, unrestrained groans.)*

**JUDGE:**
Mr. Plato, please sit—

**PLATO:**
No, truly! Consider:
Is a court the building?
Or the IDEA of the building?
And if it's the idea,
does the building participate in Court-ness,
or is it merely a shadow of—

**ARISTOTLE (standing):**
Objection.

**JUDGE:**
On what grounds?

**ARISTOTLE:**
Undefined terms.
"Court" requires formal definition before proceedings.

*(He produces a scroll.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
A court is an institution designed to adjudicate disputes
according to established rules,
precedents,
and categorical frameworks.

**JUDGE:**
Thank you, that's—

**ARISTOTLE:**
However, "institution" also requires definition.

*(He produces ANOTHER scroll.)*

An institution is a structured social arrangement
characterized by—

**DIOGENES (from the gallery, loudly):**
It's a room where people pretend they aren't guessing!

*(Laughter. The Judge slams the gavel.)*

**JUDGE:**
ORDER!

**PYTHAGORAS (also standing):**
Actually, before we proceed,
I must note:
the acoustics in this courtroom
are terrible.

**JUDGE:**
That's not—

**PYTHAGORAS:**
The resonant frequency is all wrong.
Nothing said here can be TRUE
if the harmonic ratios are off.

**JUDGE (rubbing temples):**
We haven't even started the trial yet.

**PLATO:**
Ah, but what IS starting?

**EVERYONE:**
NO!

---

## SCENE 2 — EVERYONE OBJECTS TO EVERYTHING (LA OBJECIÓN PERPETUA)

**JUDGE:**
The prosecution may proceed.

**PROSECUTOR (standing, clearing throat):**
We intend to prove—

**PLATO:**
Objection!

**JUDGE:**
...on what grounds?

**PLATO:**
This argument participates imperfectly in Justice.

**JUDGE:**
How do you know? He hasn't made the argument yet.

**PLATO:**
All arguments in the material realm
participate imperfectly in Justice.

**PROSECUTOR:**
I was going to say "good morning."

**PLATO:**
Objection sustained.

**JUDGE:**
You can't sustain your own objection!

**PLATO:**
Objection.

**JUDGE:**
TO WHAT?!

**PLATO:**
To the implication that I can't.

*(The Judge writes something on a piece of paper. It says "help me.")*

**PROSECUTOR (trying again):**
We argue that—

**ARISTOTLE:**
Objection. Undefined terms.

**PROSECUTOR:**
I HAVEN'T SAID ANYTHING YET!

**ARISTOTLE:**
Exactly.
You're about to use undefined terms.
I'm preventing it preemptively.

**JUDGE:**
That's not how objections work.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Objection.
According to my taxonomy of legal procedures—

*(He unrolls a scroll that hits the floor and keeps going.)*

—preemptive objections fall under Category 7b:
Anticipated Logical Violations.

**DIOGENES (throwing an olive):**
This is stupid!

**JUDGE:**
Mr. Diogenes, one more outburst—

**DIOGENES:**
One more? I've had like forty!

**JUDGE:**
I'm trying to be patient!

**DIOGENES:**
Why? This is already a disaster!

*(The goat, somehow in the gallery, bleats in agreement.)*

---

## SCENE 3 — EVIDENCE IS PRESENTED AND IMMEDIATELY QUESTIONED (LOS TESTIGOS IMPOSIBLES)

*(A WITNESS takes the stand. They look terrified. They should be.)*

**WITNESS:**
I saw the wheel cause—

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Objection. Correlation is not causation.

**WITNESS:**
I wasn't done with my sentence.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Objection. Your sentence was going to imply causation.

**WITNESS:**
How do you know?

**PYTHAGORAS:**
The grammatical structure—

**JUDGE:**
Let the witness speak!

**WITNESS (continuing quickly):**
I saw the wheel cause confusion which led to—

**ARISTOTLE:**
Objection. "Confusion" is not a legal category.

**WITNESS:**
What?

**ARISTOTLE:**
Confusion must be classified as:
Epistemic uncertainty,
Psychological disorientation,
or Methodological ambiguity.

Which type of confusion are you alleging?

**WITNESS:**
...the regular kind?

**ARISTOTLE:**
NOT A CATEGORY.

**WITNESS (to Judge):**
Can I leave?

**JUDGE:**
No. Please continue.

**WITNESS:**
The wheel caused people to believe—

**PLATO:**
Objection! "Believe" implies the prisoners in the cave—

**JUDGE (slamming gavel repeatedly):**
NO CAVES! NO CAVES IN MY COURTROOM!

**PLATO (quietly, to himself):**
Every room is a cave if you try hard enough.

**DIOGENES (standing up):**
The witness is done.

**JUDGE:**
The witness hasn't finished!

**DIOGENES:**
No, I mean emotionally. Look at them.

*(The WITNESS is crying silently.)*

**WITNESS:**
May I be excused?

**JUDGE (sighing):**
Yes. Yes, you may.

*(The witness leaves, grateful. Free. They run.)*

---

## SCENE 4 — THE REPAIR GUILD ARRIVES (EL GREMIO TARDE)

*(Three figures enter quietly. Practical clothes. Tools, not scrolls.)*

**REPAIR GUILD LEAD:**
We’re here about the damage.

**JUDGE:**
Who are you?

**REPAIR GUILD LEAD:**
Repair Guild.

*(Beat.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
On whose authority?

**REPAIR GUILD LEAD:**
Continuity.

**PLATO:**
What do you repair?

**REPAIR GUILD MEMBER:**
Cracks.
Miscalculations.
Systems that almost worked.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Can you restore the wheel?

**REPAIR GUILD:**
We can stabilize it.
Not sanctify it.

*(They gesture to the Ledger.)*

**REPAIR GUILD LEAD:**
Someone’s been recording harm.

No one’s been fixing it.

*(Silence. This hits.)*

---

## SCENE 5 — ARISTOTLE ALMOST WINS AGAIN (EL MOMENTO DE ESPERANZA)

**ARISTOTLE (standing, confident, organized):**
Your Honor,

if we establish clear premises,
controlled definitions,
and admissible evidence—

*(For a moment, the courtroom quiets. People lean forward. This... this might actually work.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
—we can reach a verdict.

*(People nod. Hope appears. Briefly.)*

**JUDGE:**
Please continue.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Premise One: Truth corresponds to reality.

Premise Two: Reality can be categorized.

Conclusion: Therefore, truth can be determined
through systematic categorization.

*(The courtroom is SILENT. Is this... is this actually working?)*

**PLATO (softly, from his seat):**
But what of Truth itself?

*(The moment dies.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
I just defined it.

**PLATO:**
No, you defined "truth."
Small-t truth.
But what of TRUTH?
The Form of Truth?
La Verdad Eterna?

**ARISTOTLE:**
There is no distinction—

**PLATO (standing):**
There is ONLY distinction!
Truth is not correspondence!
It's PARTICIPATION!

**ARISTOTLE:**
Participation in WHAT?

**PLATO:**
In ITSELF!

*(The courtroom erupts. Everyone is arguing. The Judge slams the gavel but no one cares.)*

---

## SCENE 6 — THE TRIAL LOOPS (EL BUCLE INFERNAL)

*(This section should GO LONG. Uncomfortably, exhaustingly long.)*

**PROSECUTOR (standing again, hours later, clearly exhausted):**
We argue that the wheel—

**DEFENSE:**
Objection.

**PROSECUTOR:**
On what grounds?

**DEFENSE:**
The wheel has been insufficiently defined.

**PROSECUTOR:**
We defined it in hour two.

**DEFENSE:**
That definition was contested in hour three.

**PROSECUTOR:**
We addressed those contestations in hour four!

**DEFENSE:**
Imperfectly.

**JUDGE (barely conscious):**
Sustained.

**PROSECUTOR:**
Your Honor, please—

**JUDGE:**
I don't know anymore.

*(PAUSE. Someone in the gallery falls asleep. Their snoring is audible.)*

**PROSECUTOR (starting over):**
The wheel, which we define as—

**DEFENSE:**
Objection.

**PROSECUTOR:**
I'M DEFINING IT!

**DEFENSE:**
Your definition assumes—

**PROSECUTOR:**
WHAT DOES IT ASSUME?!

**DEFENSE:**
That definition is possible.

*(Long, terrible silence.)*

**PROSECUTOR (quietly):**
I need a break.

**JUDGE:**
We've had seven breaks.

**PROSECUTOR:**
I need an eighth.

---

## SCENE 7 — ARISTOTLE READS THE PROCEDURAL CODE (LA TORTURA BUROCRÁTICA)

**ARISTOTLE (standing):**
Your Honor,
to ensure we follow proper procedure,
I will now read the complete legal code.

**JUDGE:**
The... complete code?

**ARISTOTLE:**
All 847 pages.

**JUDGE:**
That's not necessary—

**ARISTOTLE:**
Rule 3, Section 12, Subsection F clearly states—

*(He begins reading. Slowly. Carefully. Every. Single. Word.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
"Article One: Definitions.
Section A: Terms.
Subsection i: What constitutes a term.
Paragraph 1: A term is defined as..."

*(Two people in the gallery fall asleep. The goat falls asleep. PLATO falls asleep.)*

**ARISTOTLE (continuing, relentless):**
"...a linguistic unit of meaning,
wherein 'linguistic' refers to language,
and 'language' is defined in Appendix C,
which states..."

*(The JUDGE is writing a letter. It's a resignation letter.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
"...that language is a system of communication,
and 'system' is defined as..."

*(DIOGENES stands up to leave.)*

**JUDGE (desperately):**
Where are you going?

**DIOGENES:**
Outside. To live.

**JUDGE:**
You can't leave during proceedings!

**DIOGENES:**
Watch me.

*(He leaves. The door closing is the loudest sound anyone's heard in hours.)*

**ARISTOTLE (not stopping):**
"...a structured arrangement,
wherein 'structured' means..."

*(Cut to: THIRTY MINUTES LATER. Aristotle is still reading. Everyone is dead inside.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
"...and 'means' is defined as
the conveyance of semantic content,
where 'semantic' refers to..."

**PYTHAGORAS (waking up suddenly):**
WHAT PAGE ARE WE ON?

**ARISTOTLE:**
Forty-seven.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
Out of...?

**ARISTOTLE:**
Eight hundred forty-seven.

**REPAIR GUILD MEMBER (interrupting):**
Reading won’t repair anything.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Procedure requires—

**REPAIR GUILD LEAD:**
Procedure caused this.

*(PYTHAGORAS faints again immediately.)*

---

## SCENE 8 — THE GOAT IS CALLED AS A WITNESS (EL TESTIMONIO LEGENDARIO)

**DEFENSE (standing, desperate for anything new):**
We call our next witness:

The Goat.

**JUDGE:**
I'm sorry, what?

**DEFENSE:**
The goat has been present at every event.
It is an impartial observer.

**JUDGE:**
It's a GOAT.

**DEFENSE:**
Which means it has no philosophical agenda!

*(The courtroom considers this. It... kind of makes sense?)*

**JUDGE:**
...Fine. Bring in the goat.

*(The GOAT is led to the witness stand. It looks dignified. It has seen things.)*

**DEFENSE:**
Goat, you were present when the wheel was first revealed, yes?

*(The goat bleats once.)*

**DEFENSE:**
And what did you observe?

*(The goat bleats again. Same bleat.)*

**DEFENSE:**
Fascinating.

**PROSECUTOR:**
Objection! The witness is not responding in admissible language!

**DEFENSE:**
The bleat speaks for itself!

**PLATO (suddenly awake):**
The bleat is a shadow of True Communication!

**PYTHAGORAS:**
The bleat has a frequency!
Which means it has NUMBER!

**ARISTOTLE (standing, excited):**
I can categorize this.

*(He produces a fresh scroll.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
Bleat Taxonomy, Draft One:

Category A: Affirmative Bleats
Category B: Negative Bleats
Category C: Ambiguous Bleats
Category D: Existential Bleats

**JUDGE:**
Existential bleats?

**ARISTOTLE:**
Listen.

*(The goat bleats.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
That's a Category D.

**PROSECUTOR:**
How can you tell?

**ARISTOTLE:**
The tonal quality suggests
awareness of meaninglessness.

**DEFENSE:**
I interpreted it as Category A!

**ARISTOTLE:**
You're wrong.

**DEFENSE:**
YOU'RE wrong!

*(They begin arguing about the bleat. For AGES.)*

**PLATO:**
Perhaps the bleat participates in Bleat-Itself—

**PYTHAGORAS:**
The bleat vibrates at 437 Hz,
which when divided by the golden ratio—

**PROSECUTOR:**
The bleat is clearly saying "guilty"!

**DEFENSE:**
The bleat is clearly saying "innocent"!

**JUDGE (to the goat):**
Can you bleat again?

*(The goat stares at the Judge. Bleats once. Walks off the stand. Leaves the courtroom.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
I need to study that recording.

**JUDGE:**
There is no recording.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Then I'll have to reconstruct it from memory.

Subcategory: Remembered Bleats.

Cross-reference: Auditory Taxonomy, Section 8.

*(The Prosecutor is crying softly.)*

---

## SCENE 9 — DIOGENES ENDS THE TRIAL (LA INTERRUPCIÓN FINAL)

*(DIOGENES returns, carrying the stolen chair from Act 2. Yes. THAT chair.)*

**DIOGENES:**
I object to the trial.

**JUDGE:**
On what grounds?

**DIOGENES:**
All of them.

*(He places the chair in the center of the courtroom.)*

**DIOGENES:**
You promised answers.

You brought furniture.

*(Beat.)*

Expensive furniture.

**JUDGE:**
That's not—

**DIOGENES:**
You've been talking for HOURS.

About wheels.
About forms.
About the acoustics of goat bleats.

*(He sits in the chair.)*

**DIOGENES:**
And no one is closer to truth.

**PLATO:**
The process matters!

**DIOGENES:**
The process is torture.

**ARISTOTLE:**
We're establishing proper methodology!

**DIOGENES:**
You're establishing that you love the sound of your own voice.

*(Beat.)*

Also, this is boring.

*(He flips the witness stand. Papers scatter. Chaos. Beautiful, liberating chaos.)*

---

## SCENE 10 — THE VERDICT THAT NEVER COMES (EL VEREDICTO AUSENTE)

**JUDGE (banging gavel desperately):**
ORDER! ORDER!

*(The gavel cracks in half. The Judge stares at the broken pieces.)*

**JUDGE:**
I cannot render a verdict.

*(Silence. Complete, terrible silence.)*

**JUDGE:**
There is insufficient context.

**REPAIR GUILD LEAD:**
Then the only ethical move
is suspension — not judgment.

*(The courtroom freezes. The Chorus, visible through the windows still picketing, stops marching to watch.)*

**JUDGE:**
The evidence is contradictory.

The witnesses are unreliable.

The philosophers cannot agree on definitions.

And the goat has left the premises.

*(Longer pause.)*

**JUDGE:**
This trial is...

*(They search for the word.)*

...inconclusive.

**PROSECUTOR:**
But we've been here for DAYS!

**JUDGE:**
I know.

**DEFENSE:**
We prepared seventeen arguments!

**JUDGE:**
I heard all of them.

Twice.

**ARISTOTLE:**
If we could just establish—

**JUDGE:**
NO.

*(The Judge stands.)*

**JUDGE:**
No more establishing.

No more defining.

No more categorizing.

I resign.

*(They remove their wig. Place it on the bench. Walk out.)*

**EVERYONE:**
...

**PLATO (softly):**
Was that allowed?

**ARISTOTLE (checking his scrolls):**
There's no procedure for this.

---

## SCENE 11 — THE TRIAL COLLAPSES (EL COLAPSO TOTAL)

*(Walls retract. Marble crumbles. The flags fall. The courtroom literally deconstructs itself.)*

**NARRATOR (panicked, running onstage):**
Due to unforeseen philosophical complications,

this episode has been shortened.

*(Beat.)*

**NARRATOR:**
We apologize.

*(The Chorus, watching from outside, holds up a sign:
> **"WE TOLD YOU SO")***

**NARRATOR (to Chorus):**
Not now!

**CHORUS (chanting):**
🎵 We told you so!
We told you so!
The trial would fail,
We told you so! 🎵

---

## END OF SECTION 6 — THE PREVIEW BREAKS COMPLETELY

*(The NARRATOR looks at his cards. They are blank. Actually blank. No ink.)*

**NARRATOR (quiet, lost):**
Next time—

*(He stops. Flips through the cards. All blank.)*

**NARRATOR:**
I don't know.

*(He looks at the audience.)*

**NARRATOR:**
I genuinely don't know what happens next.

*(Lights flicker.)*

**NARRATOR:**
There might be... a machine?

Something about... algorithms?

*(He's guessing now.)*

I think someone falls in love?

*(Silence.)*

**NARRATOR (desperate):**
Anyone have the script?

*(No one answers. The courtroom is empty. Just rubble and broken scrolls and one abandoned chair.)*

*(Somewhere far away, the goat bleats. Category D. Definitely Category D.)*

*(BLACKOUT.)*

---

**END OF SECTION 6**

# SECTION 7 — THE CROSSING / EL ENCUENTRO IMPOSIBLE 🌀

## *(When the Oracle breaks reality and embarrassment becomes interdimensional)*

---

## SCENE 1 — THE ORACLE CONSULTATION (GOES VERY WRONG)

*(After the trial collapse. Everyone is exhausted, desperate, lost.)*

*(The philosophers gather at the Oracle's temple. She looks tired. Everyone looks tired.)*

**ARISTOTLE:**
We need guidance.

**PLATO:**
We need meaning.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
We need NUMBERS.

**SOCRATES (to the Oracle):**
We need... something.

What should we do?

**ORACLE (weary):**
You want me to tell you what to do?

**SOCRATES:**
Yes?

**ORACLE:**
After everything?

After the wheel?

After the trial?

After you IGNORED every warning?

**SOCRATES:**
...Yes.

**ORACLE (standing, frustrated):**
FINE!

You want answers?!

¡LES DARÉ RESPUESTAS!

*(She begins to convulse. But differently this time. More violently.)*

**ORACLE:**
I SEE—

I SEE BEYOND—

I SEE—

*(The ground shakes. The wheel starts spinning on its own.)*

**ORACLE:**
OH NO.

**ASSISTANT:**
What? What do you see?

**ORACLE:**
I SEE... ANOTHER DIMENSION!

**PYTHAGORAS:**
WHAT'S ITS RATIO?!

**ORACLE:**
I SEE... OTHER TEACHERS!

OTHER FOLLOWERS!

OTHER EMBARRASSMENTS!

*(A PORTAL opens. Swirling geometric shapes, spinning wheel, light pouring through.)*

**ORACLE:**
I SEE—

*(She's sucked toward it.)*

**ORACLE:**
—OH THIS IS BAD—

*(EVERYONE gets pulled in. Screaming. Robes flapping. The goat somehow also gets pulled in.)*

---

## SCENE 2 — ARRIVAL (WHERE ARE WE?)

*(They land in a heap. Dust. Middle Eastern sun. Completely different landscape.)*

**PLATO (looking around):**
Where... where are we?

**ARISTOTLE (checking his scrolls):**
This isn't categorized.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
The astronomical position is WRONG!

**DIOGENES (in his barrel, which somehow also got pulled through):**
Still don't care.

*(They stand, dust themselves off. Look around.)*

*(A GROUP approaches from the other direction. Twelve men in robes. Simple. Weathered.)*

**PETER (suspicious):**
Are you Romans?

**PLATO (suspicious):**
Are you Persians?

**BOTH GROUPS:**
No!

*(Awkward standoff.)*

**JOHN (softly):**
Who are you?

**ARISTOTLE:**
We are philosophers from—

**PETER:**
Philosophers? Like... wise men?

**PLATO:**
We pursue wisdom, yes.

**PETER:**
Oh good! We know someone wise!

*(He turns.)*

**PETER (calling):**
Rabbi! There are Greeks here!

---

## SCENE 3 — THE INTRODUCTION (HE KNOWS)

*(JESUS walks forward. Dusty road. Simple robe. But—)*

*(The LIGHT CHANGES. Soft. Romantic. A WIND blows his hair even though there's no wind.)*

*(He looks directly at the CAMERA and WINKS.)*

*(ROSE PETALS fall from nowhere. A rose BLOOMS in fast-motion behind him.)*

**SOCRATES (to himself):**
Oh no.

**JESUS (smiling, to Socrates):**
I've heard of you.

Greek travelers spoke of a man who asks questions until people cry.

**SOCRATES:**
And you must be the one who answers with stories until people cry.

**JESUS (laughing, warm):**
More or less.

*(He extends his hand. They shake. A VIOLIN plays. JESUS looks at the camera again and raises an eyebrow.)*

*(SOCRATES' eyes roll so hard one gets STUCK looking upward.)*

**SOCRATES (eye stuck):**
Oh.

Oh no.

**PLATO (concerned):**
Teacher? Are you alright?

**SOCRATES (eye still stuck upward):**
I can see... I can see your cave, Plato.

**PLATO (excited):**
REALLY?!

**SOCRATES:**
No. My eye is stuck.

**JESUS (trying not to laugh):**
Does that happen often?

**SOCRATES (eye stuck):**
Only around excessive drama.

*(JESUS does another telenovela pose—hand through hair, staring into the distance. MORE ROSES. A DOVE appears.)*

*(SOCRATES' other eye tries to roll but can't. He sighs.)*

**JOHN (watching them, genuinely worried):**
It’s too bad Raphael isn’t here.

*(A beat. Everyone freezes just long enough to consider this.)*

**SOCRATES:**
Why.

**JOHN:**
He could paint this.

**SOCRATES:**
This is precisely why Raphael must never be allowed anywhere near us.

**JESUS (brightening, instantly performing):**
Wait—
*(does the eye thing again, impossibly harder)*
—like this?

*(Light hits him WRONG. Halo absolutely not approved by anyone. Flowers PANIC.)*

**JESUS (to imaginary painter):**
Be honest, Raphael. You’d fix the eye, wouldn’t you?

**SOCRATES (shouting):**
HE’D MAKE IT POINT.

**JESUS:**
He always does.

*(They lock eyes.)*

**SOCRATES:**
History would never recover from this.

**JESUS (cheerfully):**
Oh, it would recover.

*(Beat.)*

**JESUS:**
Just incorrectly.

## SCENE 4 — CONFUSION SPREADS

**PETER:**
Rabbi, where are they from?

**JESUS:**
Greece.

**JOHN:**
When are they from?

**JESUS (pause):**
Good question.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Where are WE?

**JESUS:**
Palestine.

**PYTHAGORAS:**
WHEN are we?!

**JESUS:**
Also a good question.

**DISCIPLES AND STUDENTS (together):**
WHAT IS HAPPENING?!

**JESUS AND SOCRATES (together, to each other):**
This is going to be exhausting.

---

## SCENE 5 — THE DOUBLE CONVERSATION BEGINS (EMBARRASSED PARENTS)

*(The disciples and students start mixing, talking, confusing each other. Jesus and Socrates stand slightly apart, watching like tired parents at a chaotic playdate.)*

**PETER (to Plato):**
Our teacher performed MIRACLES!

**PLATO:**
Define miracle.

**PETER:**
Things that shouldn't be possible!

**PLATO:**
Then they participate imperfectly in Possibility-Itself—

**PETER:**
No! They're just miracles!

**JESUS (to Socrates, quiet):**
He's going to make it worse.

**SOCRATES (eye still stuck, to Jesus):**
Yours or mine?

**JESUS:**
Both.

*(They share a look. Suppressed smiles.)*

---

**JOHN (gentle, to Aristotle):**
Our teacher feeds thousands with five loaves.

**ARISTOTLE:**
Clarify: five loaves of what dimensions?

**JOHN:**
...Regular bread?

**ARISTOTLE:**
And the multiplication factor?

**PYTHAGORAS (SHOUTING, running over):**
FIVE?! THOUSANDS?!

WHAT'S THE RATIO?!

**JOHN (backing away):**
I... I don't know math?

**PYTHAGORAS (grabbing him by the shoulders):**
HOW CAN YOU WITNESS MULTIPLICATION WITHOUT MEASURING IT?!

**JESUS (calling over, amused):**
John, just tell him a number.

**JOHN:**
Five thousand people?

**PYTHAGORAS (COLLAPSING):**
FIVE AND FIVE THOUSAND!

THE RATIOS!

¡LOS RATIOS SAGRADOS!

**SOCRATES (to Jesus):**
Does yours faint a lot?

**JESUS:**
Does yours calculate constantly?

**SOCRATES:**
Constantly.

*(They both sigh like exhausted parents.)*

---

## SCENE 6 — THE ESCALATION (IT GETS WORSE)

**PLATO (excited, to Peter):**
Is your teacher... the Good itself?

**PETER:**
He's the Son of God!

**PLATO (GASPING):**
THE FORM OF FORMS MADE FLESH?!

**PETER:**
I... yes? Maybe?

**PLATO (weeping):**
I KNEW IT!

I KNEW FORMS COULD MANIFEST!

**PETER:**
I don't know what Forms are!

**PLATO:**
THAT'S THE TRAGEDY!

*(JESUS walks over, still telegenic. The light follows him. A flower blooms as he passes.)*

**JESUS (gently, to Plato):**
Maybe we're all just... trying our best?

*(He looks at the camera. Winks. Hair tousles in impossible wind.)*

*(SOCRATES' stuck eye twitches.)*

**SOCRATES (muttering):**
Stop doing that.
